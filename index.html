Think Before You Post

Learn how the Harmful Digital Communications Act helps keep people safe online.

01

Why the Act matters

The Harmful Digital Communications Act 2015 protects people who are bullied or harassed online.

Almost every student uses messages, social media, games or group chats. These spaces can help us connect, but they can also be used to threaten, shame or exclude people. A joke can still cause harm, and a quick post or photo can spread faster than you expect.

The Act covers harmful behaviour such as repeated abusive messages, threats, sharing private information or images without consent, and posting lies that damage someone's reputation. It reminds us that people deserve respect and privacy both online and offline.

02

What harmful communication can look like

Abusive messages

Threatening, insulting or repeatedly contacting someone to upset them.

Sharing private content

Posting someone's address, passwords, private photos or screenshots without permission.

Rumours and shaming

Spreading false stories or embarrassing posts to damage someone's reputation.

Harmful posts can cause stress, fear and long-lasting damage to confidence. A person affected may ask for support or request that harmful content be removed. In serious cases, the courts can make orders to stop communication or impose penalties. Even if you did not expect a post to hurt someone, you are still responsible for your actions.

03

Five smart online habits

  1. 01
    Pause before posting.

    Would it be kind, true and necessary?

  2. 02
    Respect privacy.

    Never share someone's details or images without permission.

  3. 03
    Do not forward harm.

    Leaving a group chat or refusing to share can stop a pile-on.

  4. 04
    Report and support.

    Use the platform's reporting tools and check on the person affected.

  5. 05
    Think face to face.

    Treat people online with the same respect you would show in person.

04

My digital goal

My goal is to stop and breathe before replying when I feel angry or upset. Pausing gives me time to consider how my words might affect someone. I can ask myself, “Would I say this face to face?” If not, I will delete the message or choose calmer words. Being a good digital citizen means making thoughtful choices, taking responsibility and helping make online spaces safer for everyone.
